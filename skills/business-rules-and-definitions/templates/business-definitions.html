Business Definitions

Name Description
transit time Is the time from the pick up to the arrival. No delivery working hours included.
  • Calculated using GeoIS (PTV), applying the Driver rule
  • Driver rule (1 driver): 2:30h driving → 45 min break → 4:30h driving → 11h break → 4:30h driving → 45 min break → 4:30h driving → 11h break → (repeat 4:30h driving → 45 min break → 4:30h driving → 11h break)
  • Driver rule (2 drivers): 16h driving → 9h break → 18h driving → 9h break → (repeat 18h driving → 9h break)
  • Counting from the delivery date, the 2:30h driving segment is the first counted (last driving event on the time range)
  • Non-ferry calculation:
    • For countries with a shared border: A to B calculation
    • For countries without a shared border: A to A/border → A/border to B (e.g. Warsaw to Brussels = Warsaw to Polish/German border + Polish/German border to Brussels)
  • Ferry calculation: distance time from start to departure ferry port (incl. driver rule) + waiting time to port arrival time on departure ferry port + ferry operation time to "ready to go" on arrival ferry port + distance time from arrival ferry port to destination (incl. driver rule)
    • Note: the driver rule is reset on arrival ferry port
  • The algorithm finds the fastest routes
  • Transit time shows the transit time in days, rounded down
  • Only workdays (Mon–Fri) are considered
  • If pick up and/or delivery falls on a non-workday, extra days are added to the delivery date
  • No driving, pick-up, or delivery on Saturdays and Sundays (global rule, overridable by a business rule — see "driver capacity")
  • Border waiting time increases the transit time
  • Business rule reference: driver capacity
  • Transit time in scheduling is based on calendar working days
  • Working days exclude Saturdays, Sundays and holidays in the delivery country
  • When calculating transit time and there is a holiday, the time until midnight is added to the calculation
driver capacity Driver is after all necessary breaks while starting a new trip.
  • A ferry, train, or driving restriction resets the driver rule
  • To be confirmed: should a ferry/train/driving restriction >11h (1 driver) or >9h (2 drivers) count as the driver having made a long break (11h for 1 driver / 9h for 2 drivers)?
  • Driver rule: determines and explains driver work, breaks, and how they apply to transit time calculation
  • Driving time for one driver: 2:30h driving → 45 min break → 4:30h driving → 11h break → 4:30h driving → 45 min break → 4:30h driving → 11h break → (repeat 4:30h driving → 45 min break → 4:30h driving → 11h break)
  • Driving time for two drivers: 16h driving → 9h break → 18h driving → 9h break → (repeat 18h driving → 9h break)
  • The driver starts the travel from 7h driving time; this is increased to 9h after the second driving break, and applies to the end of the travel
  • Any ferry, train, driving restriction, holiday, or working hours (delivery working hours) are treated as a driver's break, regardless of duration
  • Note: the driver starts the travel from 7h driving time because 2h is the required time spent at the terminal on travel preparation (papers, loading preparation, loading) — the driver is 2h ahead of the pick-up time
